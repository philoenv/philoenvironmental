The deadlines are already set.

Sourcing rules for drones tighten through 2027. A parts list that passes today may not pass at your next delivery.

Dec 2025

FCC adds foreign-produced drones and critical components to the Covered List.

Aug 2026

Drone Dominance assembly-level sourcing requirements take effect.

Jan 1, 2027

FCC carve-outs for Blue UAS and domestic products are scheduled to expire.

Feb 2027 to Aug 2027

Drone Dominance adds non-covered battery cells, GNSS receivers and US-controlled firmware.

Dates as published through October 2026. Rules have changed roughly monthly; we track them for monitoring clients.

What we check

Start with a parts-list review. Add the other services when a contract, submission or deadline calls for them.

Parts-list review

Every line traced to its manufacturer, country of origin and distributor, then checked against Section 848, the American Security Drone Act, Section 889, the 1260H list, the FCC Covered List and the 65% domestic-content test.

You get: a part-by-part compliance table, a gap report and replacement options.

Teardown verification

We take apart a production unit and compare it to the parts list: chip markings, modules, connectors and firmware sources. This is how relabeled and counterfeit parts get caught.

You get: a photographed teardown report.

Supplier and firmware review

Foreign ownership and control checks on your suppliers, and confirmation of where firmware and ground-station software are hosted and controlled.

You get: supplier risk findings.

Submission packages

Documentation assembled for Blue UAS and Green UAS submissions, or for an FCC domestic-product claim.

You get: a ready-to-submit package.

Ongoing monitoring

We watch the rule changes and re-check your parts list when they land, so a supplier swap or a new listing doesn't surprise you at delivery.

You get: monthly status updates and alerts.

How an assessment runs

A typical parts-list review takes one to three weeks, depending on its size and how much supplier documentation you already have.

  1. Send your parts list

    Your bill of materials with part numbers, plus any supplier certificates you hold. We sign an NDA first if you need one.

  2. We trace each part

    Manufacturer, country of origin and distributor for every line, checked against the rules that bind your customer.

  3. We verify the hardware

    Optional teardown of a production unit to confirm the parts list matches what's actually built.

  4. You get the report

    Findings, a compliance table and a remediation plan with replacement parts, plus a walkthrough call.

Findings are technical assessments, not legal opinions. We work alongside your counsel and can refer you to trade-compliance attorneys.
